Yeah. If we completely forget your own background and just look at **a Nigerian 400-level engineering student who has just finished exams and is entering the SIWES/internship hunt**, there is actually a pretty large set of problems around this period.

I’d break it into the **entire internship journey**, not just “find a company.”

### 1. Finding internship opportunities

This is probably the most obvious problem.

A student needs:

* Companies currently accepting SIWES students
* Companies that accept **engineering students specifically**
* Opportunities filtered by discipline — Electrical, Mechanical, Systems, Civil, Chemical, etc.
* Opportunities filtered by location — Lagos, Abuja, Ibadan, Port Harcourt, remote, etc.
* Companies that accept **SIWES specifically**, rather than only graduate internships
* Application deadlines
* Internship duration
* Stipend information
* Whether accommodation is provided
* Whether transportation is provided
* Contact/application link
* Whether the company has accepted students from their school before
* Whether the company is actually legitimate
* Information about smaller companies that don't advertise publicly

And importantly:

**A lot of students don't even know where to look.**

They hear:

> "GTBank is taking interns."

But they don't have a systematic way to discover the 50 other companies that might be suitable.

---

### 2. Knowing *what kind* of company to apply to

There's another problem underneath opportunity discovery.

A 400-level student might ask:

> "I'm studying Systems Engineering. Where am I even supposed to intern?"

They need help identifying:

* Industries relevant to their discipline
* Companies relevant to their career interests
* Roles they could realistically apply for
* Companies where they can actually learn something
* Companies that would look good on their CV
* Companies that are likely to give them actual engineering work rather than just administrative tasks

For example, someone might not know that their engineering degree could map into:

**Software → AI/ML → Embedded → Automation → Robotics → Telecoms → Control → Manufacturing → Energy → Fintech → Infrastructure**, etc.

So opportunity discovery is also **career discovery**.

---

### 3. CV / Resume

Huge one.

Many students will have:

* No CV
* A terrible CV
* A CV made from a random Canva template
* A CV that's 3 pages long
* A CV with irrelevant information
* No measurable achievements
* Projects poorly described
* No GitHub/portfolio
* No idea how to tailor the CV to a particular company

They need help with:

* Creating their first professional CV
* Engineering-specific CV structure
* ATS-friendly formatting
* Writing project descriptions
* Writing experience sections when they have no experience
* Quantifying achievements
* Choosing what to remove
* Tailoring CVs to different roles
* Reviewing/scoring their CV

---

### 4. Application letters / cover letters

Particularly in Nigeria, students may be asked for:

* Application letter
* Cover letter
* SIWES request letter
* Introduction letter
* Statement of purpose
* Motivation letter

And many don't know the difference.

They need templates **and, more importantly, personalization**.

---

### 5. School documentation

This is a completely separate category.

They may need:

* SIWES letter
* School introduction letter
* SIWES forms
* Acceptance letter
* Logbook
* Medical/fitness documentation
* Passport photographs
* Student ID
* School ID/reference details
* Placement approval
* Company acceptance documentation

And they need to know:

**"Which document do I give the company, and which one do I return to school?"**

That's surprisingly confusing for students.

---

### 6. Applying to many companies efficiently

Suppose someone wants to apply to 40 companies.

They need somewhere to track:

| Company | Role | Applied | Date | Response | Interview | Result |
| ------- | ---- | ------- | ---- | -------- | --------- | ------ |

Otherwise you get:

> "Wait, did I already apply to this company?"

They need:

* Application tracker
* Deadline reminders
* Follow-up reminders
* Status tracking
* Saved applications
* Saved job descriptions
* Contact tracking

This becomes particularly valuable when applying to 20–50 places.

---

### 7. Finding the right contact person

This is underrated.

Sometimes there isn't a public internship application page.

A student needs to figure out:

> "Who exactly do I contact?"

Potentially:

* HR
* Recruitment team
* Engineering manager
* CTO
* Operations manager
* Company founder
* Alumni working there

And then:

**How do I approach them without sounding desperate or unserious?**

---

### 8. Cold emailing / cold messaging

Another huge pain point.

Students need to know:

* Who to message
* What to say
* How long the message should be
* What to attach
* Whether to use LinkedIn, email, WhatsApp, etc.
* When to follow up
* How many times to follow up
* What to say if they don't respond

Especially for smaller Nigerian companies.

---

### 9. Networking

Many students have basically no professional network.

They need help finding:

* Alumni
* Senior students
* Previous SIWES students
* Engineers in their field
* Recruiters
* Founders
* HR professionals

And knowing:

**"How do I actually start a conversation with this person?"**

---

### 10. Interview preparation

Once applications start working, another problem appears.

Students suddenly get:

> "You're invited for an interview."

And panic.

They need:

* Common internship interview questions
* HR questions
* Technical questions
* Engineering-specific questions
* "Tell me about yourself"
* "Why should we hire you?"
* "Why this company?"
* Project questions
* Behavioral questions
* Mock interviews
* Interview feedback

---

### 11. Technical preparation

Depending on the discipline:

* Coding tests
* Aptitude tests
* Engineering fundamentals
* MATLAB
* CAD
* Electronics
* Control systems
* Circuit analysis
* Mechanics
* Thermodynamics
* Data structures
* Python
* SQL
* etc.

Basically:

**"What could this company test me on?"**

---

### 12. Aptitude tests

This deserves its own category because Nigerian companies love them.

Students may need preparation for:

* Numerical reasoning
* Verbal reasoning
* Logical reasoning
* Abstract reasoning
* Engineering aptitude
* Time-pressure tests

And practice questions.

---

### 13. Choosing between offers

Imagine someone gets:

**Company A:** ₦80k, prestigious, boring work
**Company B:** ₦30k, unknown company, actually builds interesting engineering systems
**Company C:** unpaid, but very strong mentorship

Which one should they take?

Students need help evaluating:

* Learning opportunities
* Mentorship
* Company reputation
* Stipend
* Location
* Transportation
* Accommodation
* Work environment
* Actual engineering exposure
* CV value
* Networking opportunities

---

### 14. Accommodation and transportation

This is particularly Nigerian.

A student gets:

> "You've been accepted in Lekki."

Then realizes:

> "I live in Yaba."

😭

They need to figure out:

* Where to stay
* How much accommodation costs
* Transportation options
* Daily transport cost
* Distance
* Traffic
* Whether the company provides accommodation
* Whether previous interns found accommodation nearby

For students moving from another state, this becomes even bigger.

---

### 15. Financial planning

Students need to know:

**"Can I actually afford this internship?"**

Potential costs:

* Transport
* Accommodation
* Feeding
* Work clothes
* Internet/data
* Relocation
* Documents
* Medical requirements

Sometimes the "₦50k stipend" internship is actually costing them ₦100k/month to attend.

---

### 16. Professional preparation

They've never worked in an actual organization before.

They need to learn:

* Workplace etiquette
* Email etiquette
* How to talk to managers
* How to behave in meetings
* How to ask questions
* How to report progress
* How to use Slack/Teams/etc.
* How to document work
* How to communicate professionally
* How to deal with difficult supervisors
* How to ask for feedback

---

### 17. What to learn *before* resumption

This is another big one.

A student might know:

> "I'm starting at an engineering company in September."

But:

> "What should I learn before I get there?"

They need a **pre-SIWES preparation roadmap** based on their discipline/role.

For example:

**Software intern →** Git, Python/JS, APIs, SQL, debugging
**Electrical →** MATLAB, circuit analysis, instrumentation, etc.
**Mechanical →** CAD, manufacturing, simulation, etc.

---

### 18. Setting goals for the internship

Most students just think:

> "I need to complete my SIWES."

But ideally they should enter with goals:

* Skills to acquire
* Projects to complete
* People to meet
* Technologies to learn
* Portfolio pieces to create
* Certifications to obtain
* Things to document

Otherwise six months can disappear and all they have is:

> "I worked at XYZ Ltd."

---

### 19. SIWES logbook

This is its own headache.

Students need help understanding:

* What they're supposed to record
* How often
* How to describe activities professionally
* What technical details to include
* How to avoid writing useless entries like "I resumed work and went home"
* How to structure weekly reports

---

### 20. SIWES report

At the end:

**"Now write a 40-page report about everything you did."**

Students need help with:

* Report structure
* Chapters
* Technical writing
* Diagrams
* Figures
* References
* Formatting
* Presentation
* Defense preparation

---

### 21. Internship defense

Eventually:

> "Come and defend your SIWES."

Now they need:

* Presentation structure
* Slide design
* What to talk about
* Questions they might be asked
* How to explain their project
* How to explain things they didn't personally build
* Mock defense

---

### 22. Turning the internship into career capital

This is where I think there's a **massive missed opportunity**.

The internship shouldn't just end with:

> "SIWES completed."

Students should be helped to turn it into:

**Experience → Projects → Portfolio → CV → LinkedIn → References → Future job opportunities**

For example:

> "During my internship, I automated X and reduced processing time by Y%."

That's dramatically more valuable than:

> "Intern at XYZ."

---

### 23. Getting a return offer / future opportunity

Students should also think:

> "How do I make this company want me back?"

They need advice around:

* Building relationships
* Finding mentors
* Doing excellent work
* Asking for feedback
* Asking about future opportunities
* Getting a recommendation
* Getting a reference
* Staying in touch after SIWES
* Potential graduate roles

---

# So if I zoom out...

The student's actual journey looks something like:

**"I need SIWES."**

↓
**Where can I go?**

↓
**Which companies are suitable for me?**

↓
**How do I apply?**

↓
**How do I make my CV?**

↓
**What documents do I need?**

↓
**Who do I contact?**

↓
**How do I get an interview?**

↓
**How do I pass it?**

↓
**Which offer should I accept?**

↓
**How do I afford getting there?**

↓
**What do I learn before starting?**

↓
**How do I survive the workplace?**

↓
**How do I document my SIWES?**

↓
**How do I write my report?**

↓
**How do I defend it?**

↓
**How do I turn those six months into something useful for my career?**

And that's actually a **pretty interesting product/problem space**.

The important thing is that I wouldn't immediately build "a platform for internships." That's too generic.

There are several potentially interesting products hidden inside this whole journey — **opportunity discovery, application management, student-company matching, SIWES documentation, internship preparation, or the entire end-to-end SIWES journey.**

If we're brainstorming from scratch, I'd actually explore those separately before deciding which problem is painful enough to build around.
